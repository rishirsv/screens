# Color by mood

Choose a mood word before hex values, and derive colors from tangible things in that scene. For example, mineral can draw on limestone, weathered slate and oxidized copper. One clear accent usually carries more weight than several. Add secondary accents when function or mood needs them, such as categories or semantic states, and keep their meanings consistent.

Pure white `#FFFFFF` is the everyday light ground for product, documents, marketing and mobile. A tinted ground or gray needs a reason from the mood. Otherwise use neutral grays. Pure black works with high-chroma accents or inky, nocturnal and subterranean moods; otherwise derive dark tones deliberately. Check text contrast, especially small text, against its actual surface.

Starting pairings, not presets: mineral bone and oxidized copper; maritime fog gray and deep navy; rusted graphite and rust; industrial concrete and safety orange; bookish plaster and ink; chapel slate and amethyst; candlelit amber and oxblood; botanical bone and moss; tropical palm shadow and hibiscus; alpine snow and evergreen; nocturnal wet asphalt and hot pink; phosphor black and green; dashboard black and amber LED; signage ink and chrome yellow; gallery white and cadmium red; editorial white and cobalt; hypertext white and blue; highlighter white and fluorescent yellow; brutalist white and black.

Avoid familiar combinations unless the brief calls for them: warm off-white with red, orange or terracotta; warm off-white with fluorescent color; dark navy or charcoal with electric purple, lime or teal; white with muted earth tones; and tinted warm grounds with high-chroma accents. Choose a palette for this subject, not a reflexive trend.
